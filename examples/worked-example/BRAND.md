# Brand Context

Fictional. Shows the level of specificity that changes a draft. Vague brand files produce

## Audience
- **Primary**: operations leads at 50-to-500-person companies who already run a scheduling
  tool and are deciding whether to replace it. They know the category. They do not know
  what a migration actually costs them in downtime.
- **Secondary**: the IT person who will be asked to approve it, and who cares about SSO,
  audit logs, and nothing else.
- **Active problems**: "will this import our existing shift data", "what happens to
  historical records", "can I roll back if it goes wrong".
- **Common misconceptions**: that migration is the hard part. It is not; the hard part is
  the two weeks after, when both systems are running and the data diverges.

## Editorial Rules

### Always do
- Open answer-first, in about 50 to 60 words a snippet can lift whole.
- Describe the product with plain attributes, not adjectives. Attributes get cited by
  answer engines. Adjectives get discarded.
- Cite primary sources with the date they were read, because a claim can expire.
- Carry exactly one honest admission of a limitation per article.
- Pair every diagram with the same information as prose or a table. **An SVG's content is
  invisible to answer engines.** On one build the single most extractable sentence in the
  article existed only inside the diagram.

### Never do
- No em dashes anywhere. Commas, colons, full stops. Enforced by the voice gate.
- Never claim the brand is best, leading, top, or number one.
- Never write competitively about anyone in `PARTNERS.md`.
- Never state a product fact that `FACTS.md` marks CONFLICTED or NOT PUBLISHED.
- Never publish a byline without that author's bio and photo. A named author without them
  measured roughly **8 points worse on authoritativeness than no named author at all**.
