# Partners

Who this client is never written competitively against.

Fictional. Two entries, because two is enough to show the shape and the second one shows the
case people miss: a partner who is also, genuinely, a competitor.

## Northwind Data

- **Relationship:** supplies the pricing feed the whole product depends on. The integration
  is not a feature, it is the plumbing.
- **How they may appear in copy:** named plainly as the data source. Naming a supplier is a
  provenance attribute, and attributes get cited by answer engines. Adjectives do not.
- **Never:** as a competitor, as a comparison target, or in any "alternatives to" framing.
- **Domains:** northwinddata.com, docs.northwinddata.com

## Calyx Systems

- **Relationship:** reseller partner. **Also a competitor in one segment**, which is exactly
  why the rule has to be written down rather than left to judgement.
- **How they may appear in copy:** as an integration, and as a cited source.
- **Never:** in a comparison page, a "best X" listicle they would appear in, or a page
  targeting the segment where the two overlap. When in doubt the answer is no, and the
  client decides, not the writer.
- **Domains:** calyxsystems.com

## What an incomplete list costs

This does not fail loudly. It surfaces weeks later as a keyword you have to drop after the
research is already done, or worse, as a published page positioning the client against a
company it depends on to function.

On the account this engine was built from, a partner list blocked whole article types, and
three partners turned up ranking on target SERPs inside a single week. `serp_gate.py` reads
this file for exactly that reason.

Ask the client for domains, not just names. The SERP check matches on domains.
